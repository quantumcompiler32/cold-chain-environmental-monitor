# Vaccine cold-chain dashboard: impact sources

*Research date: 28 July 2026. Sources are first-party guidance, standards, or government publications. Claims below are paraphrased; links point to the source that owns each claim.*

## How to read this note

The prototype can demonstrate earlier detection, an auditable temperature history, and maintenance triage. It cannot by itself establish vaccine viability, authorize use after an excursion, prove avoided waste, or claim a financial return. The impact matrix at the end therefore separates source-backed effects from demo assumptions that would require field measurement.

## 1. Why cold-chain failure and temperature monitoring matter

| Source-backed finding | What it means for the prototype | Limits / wording to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WHO reports that studies in industrialized and developing countries found vaccines commonly exposed to damaging temperatures, especially sub-zero exposure. WHO describes systematic temperature monitoring across the supply chain as the simplest way to identify these risks and prevent damaging excursions. [WHO, *Temperature monitoring study: a fully documented process to detect weaknesses in the supply chain* (WHO/IVB/18.04, 2018)](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-IVB-18.04) | A dashboard that shows temperature history, excursion intervals, and affected segments addresses a documented visibility gap. | This is evidence that exposure occurs and monitoring identifies risk—not evidence that this prototype prevents a particular number of excursions. |
| WHO's evidence brief says temperatures outside recommended ranges may reduce potency, leave children inadequately protected, contribute to outbreaks, and create monetary loss; it recommends using monitoring data to identify where corrective action is needed. [WHO evidence brief PDF (p. 1)](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/272856/WHO-IVB-18.04-eng.pdf?ua=1) | Use “protects potency / supports corrective action” as the impact language. | The dashboard does not make a clinical disposition decision; escalation to the immunization programme or manufacturer remains necessary. |
| UNICEF states that vaccines can lose potency after excessive heat or freezing, so monitoring during storage and transportation is vital. Data from monitoring devices informs decisions such as use within an approved excursion range, a shake test when freezing is suspected, or discard. [UNICEF Supply Division, E006 temperature-monitoring-device procurement guidance](https://www.unicef.org/supply/documents/e006-temperature-monitoring-devices) | An excursion record and “hold / investigate” workflow can preserve the evidence needed for an authorized decision. | Do not present an alert as proof that a dose is safe or unsafe; vaccine-specific guidance and qualified staff decide disposition. |
| CDC's July 2026 toolkit says exposure outside recommended ranges may reduce potency, decrease protection, and require revaccination. It states that proper storage and handling prevent unnecessary revaccination, minimize vaccine wastage, and help ensure a viable supply. [CDC, *Vaccine Storage and Handling Toolkit* (July 2026), pp. 3–5](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/downloads/storage-handling-toolkit.pdf) | The “impact” panel may describe reduced risk of undetected exposure, unnecessary revaccination, and preventable wastage as intended outcomes. | These are program goals from CDC guidance, not measured outcomes for this demo. No avoided-dose or dollar figure should be shown without site data. |

## 2. Official guidance on continuous monitoring and data logging

| Guidance | Design implication | Limits / wording to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CDC says every vaccine storage unit needs a reliable temperature-monitoring device; it recommends a continuously monitoring digital data logger (DDL) recording no less frequently than every 30 minutes. A DDL records all preset-interval temperatures and shows how long the unit was outside range, unlike a simple min/max thermometer. [CDC, *Pink Book*, Chapter 5](https://www.cdc.gov/pinkbook/hcp/table-of-contents/chapter-5-vaccine-storage-and-handling.html); [CDC toolkit, pp. 12–13](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/downloads/storage-handling-toolkit.pdf) | A prototype can show continuous points, excursion duration, threshold alarms, calibration status, and downloadable history; “30-minute maximum interval” is a CDC recommendation for vaccine storage, not a universal sensor setting for every use case. | The display is illustrative unless the deployed device is calibrated, correctly placed, and validated for the product's temperature range. |
| CDC says accurate history reflecting actual vaccine temperatures is critical to quality and potency; it recommends buffered probes, calibration testing, backup devices, and reviewing DDL data. [CDC toolkit, pp. 12–13](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/downloads/storage-handling-toolkit.pdf) | Show probe/device health, calibration due date, data gaps, and backup-device status alongside the temperature chart. | A software chart cannot compensate for a bad probe, poor placement, sensor drift, or missing data. |
| WHO's remote-temperature-monitoring brief says RTM systems continuously monitor vaccine temperatures, trigger alarms when values leave the appropriate range, and alert health workers for faster reaction to prevent damage. [WHO, *Perception of health workers on the use of remote temperature monitoring systems* (WHO/IVB/18.03, 2018)](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-IVB-18.03) | Alert routing, acknowledgement, escalation, and response-time fields are defensible dashboard workflows. | Faster reaction is a mechanism described by WHO, not a quantified response-time improvement for the prototype. |
| WHO's 30-day recorder brief says recorders store excursions and provide evidence of what happened between manual readings. [WHO, *Perception of health workers on the use of 30-day temperature recorders* (WHO/IVB/18.02, 2018)](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-IVB-18.02) | Include “between checks” history, not only current/min/max cards. | A 30-day retention window is a device characteristic in this guidance; the prototype should label any other retention period as a product decision. |
| WHO's Vaccine Management Handbook says monitoring, recording, and reporting temperatures throughout the supply chain documents exposure, helps maintain vaccine quality, prevents waste from heat/freezing, and helps detect equipment failures so corrective action can be taken quickly. [WHO, *How to monitor temperatures in the vaccine supply chain* (WHO/IVB/15.04, 2015), PDF pp. 1–2](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/183583/WHO_IVB_15.04_eng.pdf?sequence=1) | The dashboard can preserve an end-to-end record and tie an excursion to a corrective-action task. | This describes the monitoring program's intended function; it is not a measured effect of this software. |
| WHO's E006 prequalification page distinguishes remote systems (sensor networks, local/remote servers, alarms) and says routine manual recording is known to be unreliable at some levels. It also describes equipment-monitoring systems (EMS) with standardized logger data and remote communications. [WHO PQS, E006 Temperature Monitoring Devices](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/immunization-devices/e006-temperature-monitoring-devices) | The dashboard's event feed, remote alerting, and structured asset/event data align with the direction of WHO's E006/EMS architecture. | E006 is procurement/specification guidance. A prototype is not WHO-prequalified equipment and should not imply compliance. |

## 3. Predictive maintenance / condition monitoring: cautious support

| Source | Source-backed claim | Safe prototype interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [ISO 17359:2018, *Condition monitoring and diagnostics of machines — General guidelines*](https://www.iso.org/standard/71194.html?browse=ics) | ISO 17359 gives general procedures for establishing a condition-monitoring programme for machines and applies to all machines; ISO lists the 2018 edition as current after 2023 confirmation. | Use it as a vocabulary/architecture reference for condition-monitoring workflows, not as evidence that this dashboard or a refrigerator complies with the standard. |
| [NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, MA-6(2) Predictive Maintenance (p. 170)](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-53r5) | NIST defines predictive maintenance as periodic or continuous condition monitoring used to schedule maintenance before performance crosses a threshold; inspections are often in-service, minimizing disruption, and predictive maintenance can yield higher reliability and substantial cost savings. NIST's MA-6(3) describes transferring predictive-maintenance data to a maintenance-management system to trigger planning, execution, and reporting. | A dashboard may present a *maintenance signal* (for example, repeated excursions, recovery time, power loss, or compressor-related indicators) and create/track a work order. Label this “condition-based triage” unless a validated model supports a prediction. | 
| CDC's vaccine-specific toolkit requires regular maintenance of storage units and monitoring devices, documentation of maintenance and repairs, and routine work to ensure equipment functions at maximum efficiency. It says repeated alarms should not be disconnected until the cause is identified; persistent alarms warrant repair-technician assessment for repair or replacement. [CDC toolkit, pp. 19–20](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/downloads/storage-handling-toolkit.pdf) | Maintenance status, repeated-alarm history, assigned owner, and repair/replace outcome are grounded in vaccine cold-chain operations. | This is prescribed maintenance and troubleshooting guidance, not proof that an algorithm can predict failure. |
| [U.S. Department of Energy, Energy Management Information System capabilities](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/energy-management-information-system-capabilities) | DOE says condition-based and predictive maintenance detect onset of performance degradation so operators can correct issues before failure; real-time equipment data can support reduced failure rates, avoided labor, and avoided unnecessary maintenance. | This supports the mechanism in an impact matrix: earlier signal → investigation/work order → potential avoided failure. DOE's examples are building/HVAC systems, so transfer to vaccine refrigerators is an inference requiring validation. |
| [NIST, *Procedural Guide for System-Level Impact Evaluation … for Condition Monitoring Systems* (2021)](https://www.nist.gov/publications/procedural-guide-system-level-impact-evaluation-industrial-artificial-intelligence) | NIST frames condition-monitoring systems as software/devices that alert users to changing asset conditions and provides a procedure for evaluating system-, facility-, and broader impacts. | Measure baseline and post-pilot outcomes (alert precision, response time, downtime, excursions, maintenance actions, and wastage) before claiming impact. |
| WHO's E006 page notes growing interest in monitoring voltage, humidity, compressor function, and other equipment parameters; it says those data could enable remote failure analysis and ideally predictive failure responses, reducing equipment downtime and vaccine risk. [WHO PQS E006](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/immunization-devices/e006-temperature-monitoring-devices) | It is reasonable for a prototype to show a future-facing “equipment health” lane and explain which extra signals would be needed. | WHO uses “could,” “ideally,” and “potential”; do not turn this into a guaranteed failure-avoidance or ROI claim. |

## Impact matrix for the prototype (evidence vs. assumptions)

| Dashboard capability | Evidence-backed mechanism | Demo assumption / validation needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous temperature history and excursion duration | CDC/WHO say continuous logging reveals duration and events missed between manual checks. | Sensors are calibrated, correctly placed, connected, and representative of vaccine temperature. |
| Alarm, acknowledgement, escalation, and response log | WHO says RTM alarms can alert staff for faster reaction; CDC requires documented excursion actions. | Staff receive and act on alerts; measure acknowledgement and time-to-action in a pilot. |
| Affected-stock “hold / investigate” view | UNICEF and CDC say temperature data informs use, testing, isolation, or disposition decisions. | Inventory, lot, and excursion linkage is complete; authorized clinical/programme decision-maker reviews it. |
| Equipment-health / maintenance queue | NIST, DOE, ISO, and WHO support condition monitoring and maintenance planning before failure as a mechanism. | Repeated excursions or other signals predict a real equipment fault with acceptable false-alert burden; validate against work orders and failures. |
| Claimed wastage, downtime, or dollar savings | Sources support these as possible outcomes or program goals. | Baseline, denominator, time horizon, and counterfactual must be measured at a real site. This prototype should show no ROI percentage or absolute savings number. |

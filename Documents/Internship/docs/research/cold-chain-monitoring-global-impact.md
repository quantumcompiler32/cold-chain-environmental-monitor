# Why cold-chain monitoring matters: global impact figures

*Research date: 28 July 2026. Figures retain the source's reporting year.*

## The problem a temperature controller does not solve

A refrigerator's thermostat controls its local air temperature; it does not provide a continuous, auditable record that the product stayed safe throughout storage and transport. Door openings, power failures, poor airflow, equipment faults, sensor drift, and transfers between facilities can still cause an excursion. Monitoring turns this hidden risk into an alert and a record an operator can act on.

For vaccines, this distinction is explicit in WHO/UNICEF guidance: vaccines can lose potency after excessive heat or freezing, and monitoring data supports decisions to use, test, or discard stock. Nearly all routine-programme vaccines use the +2 °C to +8 °C cold chain; a safe return to that range does **not** by itself prove that no damaging excursion occurred.

## Demo-ready facts

- **526 million tonnes (12%) of global food production** are lost directly because effective refrigeration is unavailable. The UNEP–FAO analysis estimates that developing countries reaching developed-country cold-chain infrastructure could save **144 million tonnes a year**. [UNEP/FAO, 2022](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/press-release/amid-food-and-climate-crises-investing-sustainable-food-cold-chains)
- FAO's latest SDG estimate puts food loss from post-harvest through retail at **13.3% globally in 2023**. Losses are **25.4% for fruit and vegetables** and **23.0% in sub-Saharan Africa**, showing why perishable products and infrastructure gaps matter. [FAO SDG data portal](https://www.fao.org/sustainable-development-goals-data-portal/data/indicators/1231-global-food-losses)
- The food lost because refrigeration is lacking emitted an estimated **1 gigatonne CO2e in 2017**—about **2% of global GHG emissions**. The full food cold chain, including cooling equipment and food losses, accounts for about **4% of global GHG emissions**. [UNEP/FAO, 2022](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/press-release/amid-food-and-climate-crises-investing-sustainable-food-cold-chains)
- Efficient monitoring is also an energy and climate lever: UNEP projects that, under current policy, global cooling capacity will **triple by 2050**, its electricity use will **more than double**, and cooling emissions will reach **6.1 GtCO2e** in 2050 (more than 10% of global emissions). [UNEP Global Cooling Watch](https://www.unep.org/topics/energy/cooling/about-cooling)
- **1.05 billion tonnes of food** were wasted at retail, food service, and household stages in **2022**, while **783 million people** faced hunger. This is broader than cold-chain failure, but it gives the human context for reducing preventable perishables loss. [UNEP Food Waste Index 2024](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/press-release/world-squanders-over-1-billion-meals-day-un-report)
- WHO says vaccine temperature-monitoring studies have found exposure to potentially damaging temperatures—including sub-zero exposure—to be common, and that end-to-end monitoring identifies risk points and prevents excursions. [WHO evidence brief, 2018](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-IVB-18.04)
- WHO/UNICEF state that vaccines may lose potency after excessive heat or freezing; monitoring logs enable decisions on whether stock can be used, requires a test, or must be discarded. [UNICEF temperature-monitoring guidance](https://www.unicef.org/supply/documents/e006-temperature-monitoring-devices)

## Suggested IIT Madras explanation

> Refrigeration automates cooling, but it does not automate *proof*. Our system verifies that every storage unit and hand-off stayed in range, detects an excursion early, tells the operator which stock may be affected, and preserves the evidence needed to respond. That reduces preventable food and vaccine loss while helping operators find inefficient or failing equipment before it wastes more energy.

## Important scope note

This prototype supports monitoring and operator response; it does not make clinical vaccine-release decisions. Also, not all global food loss is caused by refrigeration failure—the 526-million-tonne figure is the specific UNEP–FAO estimate attributed to insufficient refrigeration, whereas FAO's 13.3% is the broader food-loss indicator.
